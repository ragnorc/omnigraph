# Spike: the GQ 3 grammar of RFC PR #1202

**Branch:** `spike/gq3-1202` (from `main` `f728f01e`), throwaway code.
**RFC head reviewed:** `7c44b23d` (`docs/rfcs/2026-10-28-gq-3-grammar.md` and its evaluation asset), with both review comments on the PR and the open RFCs it names (#792, #793, #805, #884, #1015).
**Question:** is the design correct before acceptance, above all the claim that GQ makes one incompatible change (edition 3) and then only additive ones because every later capability has a reserved slot?

## Overall verdict

**Correct with amendments. No blocker found that forces a second grammar break, but the claim as written is stronger than the evidence, and five non-grammar contracts must be settled before the freeze or they become the second break.**

1. **The one-break claim holds for the grammar.** I wrote 60 later capabilities (from this RFC, its evaluation, #792, #793, #805, #884, #1015 and the Lance primitives) as the queries they need. None changes the meaning of a well-typed edition-3 query. 16 parse into a slot and are refused with its owner, 14 are registry rows or options, 5 already work, 24 need a new grammar production, and 1 already parses with another, ill-typed meaning.
2. **The "every later capability has a reserved slot" claim does not hold.** The 24 new productions include pattern-scoped writes, which the Motivation says have slots, plus unions and `is T` (#884), qualified edges, `use` and `namespace` blocks (#1015), arithmetic (weighted fusion, computed writes), partial objects, `at $commit`, `nulls last`, multivectors and new base types. Each one starts at a token edition 3 already refuses, so each is additive. Compatibility comes from PEG ordered choice, contextual keywords, `$`-variables and the `@` namespace, not from the slots. The slots buy a committed spelling and a better diagnostic, not compatibility. The RFC should say so and drop the sentence that a later capability is "a registry row, a plan node, or the filling of a slot".
3. **The rewriter and its IR-equality proof work, against the review's prediction**, provided an arm reference is resolved to its call before IR is built. 1,818 GQT declarations and 2,968 generated reads migrate and compile to identical typed IR. A swapped incoming triple also lowers to identical IR (4 of 4 corpus cases and a negative control).
4. **Five contracts would break later if frozen as written, or are scheduled to break by other RFCs:**
   - the score column's type and name;
   - what `having` over a score means;
   - #793's three new refusals, which edition 3 accepts;
   - #792's removal of `search`, `match_text` and `fuzzy`, and its T27 tightening;
   - the edition-3 refusal of an incoming triple, which the compatibility list omits.
   Each needs a decision now (amendments A1 to A5 below).
5. **The RFC's pest sketch has defects** that would make the frozen grammar refuse its own examples or today's text (amendments G1 to G8). All are small fixes, and the spike's grammar applies them.

### Amendments

- **A1, score columns.** Freeze what the engine publishes. On Lance that is a non-null `Float32` named `<var>._distance` or `<var>._score` (`F64` for `rrf`), not `F64?` named after the arm. Alternatively, decide now that edition 3 renames the column to `<var>.<arm>`, and list that as part of the break: 2 GQT projections change name.
- **A2, `having` over a score.** State that it means "the top `limit` rows of the population passing the predicate". That is what the engine already does: the overfetch ladder widens until `limit` rows pass, and it equals Lance `distance_range`. Freeze it in edition 3.
  - Under `rrf`, an arm's own score cannot be projected or filtered today (T33), because the fusion carries only the fused column. "Scores are values" therefore needs `RankFuse` to carry each arm's column (engine work), or `having` over a fused arm stays a slot.
  - `having` also needs its own type rule. Today's Boolean-return rule (T58) refuses a group key beside an aggregate (`count($d) >= 2 and $p.name != "x"`).
- **A3, #793's refusals.** Move them into edition 3, or have #793 drop them. Edition 3 accepts duplicate arms, arms on two bindings, and a `nearest` arm in an aggregate query; refusing them afterwards is a break.
- **A4, registry rows are never removed.** #792 removes `search`, `match_text` and `fuzzy` at "the next major", and its T27 tightening refuses `bm25` over `@index` without `@analyzed`. Under one break, either #792 lands those in edition 3 or the rows stay for good, with the formatter canonicalising to `match_terms`. Record the choice in both RFCs. #805's `(3, 0)`/`(4, 0)` numbering also has to go, as the review says.
- **A5, the edition-3 incoming-triple refusal (`T5`) is a break the compatibility list omits.** The rewriter table needs a swap row. With it, the proof passes (the 4 corpus cases inside `not`/`exists`/`count` blocks in `issue_659_compiler_scopes.gqt`). The RFC must also say what a directed alternation with mixed member orientations is. It resolves per member today; the "written against the schema" rule does not say.

### Grammar fixes (the spike's grammar applies each; tests pin the RFC's literal rule failing)

- **G1.** `call_args`: `positional ~ ("," ~ positional)* ~ ("," ~ option)*` never parses an option after a positional (`rrf($a, $b, k: 60)`), because PEG takes `k` as a bare-name positional and does not backtrack. Fix: `positional = { !(ident ~ ":") ~ expr }`.
- **G2.** `binding` with arguments: `$p: Person` followed by a parenthesised filter on the next line (legal edition-2 text) is read as view arguments, a silent change of meaning. Fix: the argument list must touch the name (`Name(`).
- **G3.** `undirected_edge = "<" ~ edge_sel ~ ">"` drops today's `<knows | likes>`, which the typed edge alternation RFC accepted. Fix: admit `edge_alternatives` inside `< >`.
- **G4.** Deleting `date_lit`/`datetime_lit` breaks `[date("…"), …]` list literals (`issue_801_in_predicate_over_a_list.gqt`). Fix: a list item is a literal or a constant call.
- **G5.** `count_call` admits `distinct` only after `count`, so `sum(distinct $x)` changes from T57 to a parse error (`issue_954_count_distinct.gqt`). Fix: any aggregate name, with T57 kept in the type checker.
- **G6.** `annotation_args` is undefined. Use `=` for named arguments (the `.pg` spelling), allow any order and several positionals (`@valid(a, b)`, `@analyzed(analyzer="…")`), meta-fields and variables (`@if_commit($c)`). The evaluation's `@budget(rows: 10)` then does not parse; one spelling has to win, and `=` is the one stored `.pg` text uses.
- **G7.** `prop_access` is one level, so the frozen meta-fields `@distance`/`@score` "of a retrieve arm's column" have no spelling (`$d.vec.@distance`). Either freeze a path rule or drop the two names.
- **G8.** The arrow recognizer must be in `clause` before `traversal` (the review is right). Some RFC examples do not parse as intended:
  - `$p: Person <owns | ownedBy> $d: Doc` is a parse error. Traversal endpoints are bare variables.
  - `$t: @Type @property $p: @Property` and `$e: @Edge @from $t` parse as a binding, a bare `@…` filter and a second clause, not a traversal.

## What I could not verify

- **The competence instrument** (the RFC's first gate). Its task set is outside the repository; I did not run it. The parser it needs is on the branch: `gq 3;` sources parse and refuse slots.
- **The edition-2 boot rewrite**, `cluster plan` output, the release-after refusal, envelope fields, explain/bound-plan version bumps, named score columns in explain, and parameter defaults. None was built.
- **The fleet's stored queries.** The corpus is the repository: GQT cases, the guides, the skill and `.gq` fixtures. 43 doc blocks have no schema, so they were checked for parse only.
- **Each of the 24 new productions.** I showed each form is refused today, so adding it at its first novel token is additive. I did not implement them. Arithmetic needs a lookahead against `$a * $b`.
- **The RFC's T22 narrowing** (`<owns | ownedBy>` per member). I did not implement it.
- **ANN behaviour of `having` under a real IVF index at scale.** The Lance test graph is 6 rows, where the ladder's search is exact.
- **S3 and Azure, and release-build timing.** Everything ran on local files and a debug build.
- **Workspace-wide test suites.** I ran the spike tests plus the full compiler and planner suites (green). I did not run the canonical workspace test graph, GQT, clippy or DST. The `QueryIR`/`QueryDecl` field additions compile across the workspace.
